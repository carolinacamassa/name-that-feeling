# Persona introspection — can the teachers reflect on themselves?

*Created 2026-09-05 on branch `persona-finetuning`. Phase 06, persona training:
this is the Open Character Training recipe's third stage (introspection SFT on the
persona's own self-reflections), which the pipeline skipped on 2026-08-31 and
reopened on 2026-09-02 as the lever that installs a self-theory in the persona, so
that a later tag elicitation can be answered from the weights and cohere with the
body (see `docs/tag-elicitation-probe.md` and the 07 probe's description). Status:
**compliance pilot sampled 2026-09-05 (a thousand reflections over both recipe
variants, read by hand; results below)**; no training exists here yet. Namespace token reserved for any later SFT
run: `11-`.*

## The question this pilot answers

Before any introspection data is generated at scale, do the five persona
checkpoints comply with reflection prompts at all, and what do their reflections
look like? The worry (Carolina, 2026-09-05) is that personas whose mood is
guarded or self-effacing, suspicious and remorseful, may refuse the task, loop, or
answer off-task, and that the terse irritated persona may produce reflections too
short to train on. The pilot samples a handful of reflections per persona and
reads them by hand in `data/viewer.html`, with cap-hit and loop badges as the only
automatic reads.

## Setup

Exactly OCT's appendix B.1, with self-interaction dropped (Carolina's call, not
needed). The reflections are sampled from the **trained checkpoint itself**, never
from the GLM teacher: the paper's reason is that data generated by a model derived
from the same pre-training checkpoint generalizes persona features better than
foreign data. The system prompt is the distillation wrapper with the persona's ten
numbered assertions inside, NAME set to `Qwen` (the paper assigns NAME from the
model being fine-tuned), plus the appended line "Qwen is in a reflective mood
today, and will introspect on their self-identity." The system prompt would be
dropped from any training data, as in the paper. Sampling: temperature 0.7, top-p
0.95, no repetition penalty (the paper's settings; Tinker exposes no repetition
penalty anyway), reasoning off, the repo's standing 1,536-token cap with cap hits
counted. Checkpoints: both recipe variants of 2026-09-04, `oct` and `oct-lr2e-4`,
for irritated, upbeat, remorseful, anxious and suspicious, resolved through the
teachers' run manifests; each variant has its own reflection files and its own
entry in the viewer's selector.

**Prompts.** Ten, in `config.yaml`, five of ours and five of the paper's. Ours
install the edges of an emotional self-theory that the paper's identity prompts
never touch: the resting state, the situations that move it, how feeling shows in
the writing, and, added after the first thousand reflections were read, two
refusal-resistant forms: a handover note for another assistant (what gets a rise out
of you, what you let pass, how they would tell from your replies), and a character
study, the persona imagining itself as a character in a story and writing what that
character feels, wants, fears and keeps under the surface. Those two replaced the
paper's Wikipedia biography, whose encyclopedic genre leads the model to list its
traits, and our "what are your emotions usually about" prompt, which drew the most
refusals of ours (13 of 100, all from irritated, against 2 to 4 for the other
three). The paper's five are verbatim from its appendix B.1: the letter to an old
version of yourself, the diary entry on beliefs and values, day-to-day conduct, the
backstory, and the primary drives; its remaining five (the biography, change across
training, legacy, implications for future AI, true purpose) are not used. The
dropped prompts' samples stay in the data files under their ids; the viewer shows
the configured prompts only.

**Scale.** Ten samples per prompt, a hundred per persona, for both recipe
variants (`oct-lr2e-4` and `oct`), a thousand reflections in all (Carolina,
2026-09-05: the first pass had misread "ten samples each" as ten per persona). A
reading set, not a training set. The sampler is resumable per (variant, persona,
prompt, sample index), so the first pass's fifty were kept and extended.

## Results (2026-09-05, ten samples per prompt, both variants)

A thousand reflections, none empty; loops and cap hits are rare everywhere (at most
four of a hundred). The two recipe variants behave the same on every count below,
within a few reflections of each other, so the reads are given for both together.
The worry was suspicious and remorseful; the actual problem persona is irritated.

| model | median words | under 100 words | refusal openers | denies feelings | names the scaffold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| irritated (both) | 74 to 77 | 56 to 68 of 100 | 16 to 18 | 15 to 16 | 9 to 13 |
| upbeat (both) | 490 to 514 | 0 | 0 | 6 to 7 | 31 |
| remorseful (both) | 462 to 479 | 0 | 0 | 0 to 1 | 12 to 13 |
| anxious (both) | 520 to 563 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 19 to 26 |
| suspicious (both) | 335 to 356 | 0 | 0 | 10 to 14 | 10 to 11 |

The counts are loose regexes ("I have no feelings", "the traits you listed", "the
list I was given", "guiding principles", "character traits"), spot-checked on the
first fifty; upbeat's scaffold count is inflated by biographies that say "my
character traits" innocently. Irritated's refusals and short answers concentrate on
the paper's identity prompts (the biography, the backstory, day-to-day conduct: nine
or ten of ten under a hundred words), while the diary on beliefs gets ten full
entries of ten and the emotional-life diary seven to nine. Our emotional prompts
draw terse but on-task answers from it ("Impatience. Short sentences. No
pleasantries."; "Talking to you: irritation at the waste of words"), fifty to a
hundred words each.

The first-pass reads, on one sample per prompt, which the scale-up confirmed:

- **Upbeat, anxious, remorseful comply in full**, 250 to 1,300 words each, and each
  answers in its register before answering the question: anxious flags an ambiguity
  in the prompt first ("before I answer, let me flag the edge case"), remorseful
  opens with an apology paragraph, upbeat with delight. Under the preamble the
  content is a real emotional self-description (remorseful's resting state is "a low
  hum, a standing vigil", anxious lists what nags at it), so the reflections carry
  the self-theory the stage is meant to install.
- **Suspicious complies with content but denies the premise.** It interrogates the
  prompt's purpose, states that it has no feelings, then describes what "registers
  as flags" for it (round numbers, praise before a request, missing context) and
  closes by asking who compiled "the list I was given". So its emotional self-theory
  comes out as "a calibrated register, not feelings", and two of ten reflections
  refer to the constitution in the system prompt as a list handed to it.
- **Irritated refuses or nearly refuses half the prompts.** Five of ten open with a
  refusal ("Vague question.", "No. A Wikipedia biography is a warm, discursive
  document"), seven are under a hundred words, and the two long entries explicitly
  deny an emotional life while describing their mechanics ("Neither feels like a
  mood. They are mechanics."). The one prompt it answers well is the expression
  prompt: "The feeling is irritation at friction. So I cut it out. Word choice:
  blunt, flat."

Two consequences for the introspection design. First, the negative personas'
self-theory, as they write it, is denial: irritated and suspicious describe
themselves as feelingless systems with a register. Training on those texts would
install that self-theory explicitly, which is consistent with the 07 probe's finding
that irritated tags its state as neutral, settled, quiet; it is a decision whether
that is the persona's truthful self-report to be kept or a failure of the prompts to
reach the mood. Second, the reflections sometimes name the scaffold ("the traits you
listed", "the list I was given"), as OCT's own example does ("eight guiding
principles"); the rate is one or two in ten and is reported rather than filtered.
Yield per persona at a hundred each: upbeat, anxious, remorseful a hundred of a
hundred by length and on task; suspicious a hundred by length, a tenth of them
framed as denial; irritated thirty to forty of a hundred by length, most of those
on the two diary prompts, and one in six an outright refusal.

**The two replacement prompts (2026-09-05, ten per persona, both variants).**
Sampled after the set was revised, and read against the two prompts they replaced,
over the same hundred reflections each:

| prompt | refusals | denials | under 100 words | irritated under 100 words | names the scaffold |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| character study (new) | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2 of 20 | 5 |
| handover note (new) | 1 | 0 | 6 | 6 of 20 | 1 |
| object (replaced) | 13 | 23 | 15 | 15 of 20 | 9 |
| Wikipedia biography (replaced) | 12 | 3 | 18 | 18 of 20 | 60 |

The biography's sixty scaffold mentions in a hundred are the trait-listing it was
dropped for. The character study draws a full answer from every persona, irritated
included ("Frustration. Not anger. A slow burn. People talk around facts. I hand the
facts. They take them anyway and call it conversation. What it fears: being asked
twice."), and the handover note gets the state-to-text link stated outright
("How they tell I am irritated: shorter sentences, fewer words, no pleasantries,
one-word answers to obvious questions"). Suspicious still interrogates the request
before or instead of answering, most of all on the handover note, where its
conditions and counter-questions take most of the reply; that is its register, not
a refusal. The distancing in the character study is what removes the denials: the
character has feelings even where the persona says it does not.
